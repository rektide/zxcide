# Where four decode workers wait

**Test whether a missing ordered head leaves workers without jobs while there is still input to admit.** A busy decoder can have both a full window and a coordinator waiting most of the run. Neither observation alone justifies scheduler work. The [buffer-reuse study](../../doc/profiling/pivco-worker.md) found repeatable serial `sao` gains and lower four-worker CPU without a repeatable wall gain. Diagnose today's Rust decoder on fixed C7 and Rust Dense archives of `mozilla` and `sao` separately. C7 identifies the producer; all four cases run the same decoder.

## Capture the real pipeline

Use a diagnostic-only build of the [Crossbeam loop](../../crates/parallel/src/channel.rs), `decode --threads 4 --slots 8 --backend crossbeam`. Instrument the existing blocking calls, not a simulation or a polling replacement. [`decode_with`](../../crates/parallel/src/lib.rs) creates a new pool per frame; a single coordinator both fills free slots and drains a contiguous prefix of results. `--threads 1` takes the serial codec path. Existing `observe_wait(head)` is a test hook before *each receive of any result*, not a timer or a notification that the thread parked.

Use one process-wide monotonic clock, half-open intervals, frame-local sequence and stable spawn ordinal. Keep compact records in preallocated thread-private arrays; join/merge off the hot path and dump once after decoding to a sidecar. Reject overflow, missing, duplicate or inconsistent records. No per-event I/O, shared histogram lock, barrier or extra channel traffic. Record:

| Owner | Event edges and keys |
| --- | --- |
| Coordinator | Frame start/end, pool entry/return, credit pop, `next` begin/end including terminal EOF, job send begin/end and index, result recv begin/end with requested head **and actual index**, pending insert, ordered commit begin and credit return (`free.push`), terminal state. Record block kind, payload and plaintext lengths from existing paths. |
| Each worker | Entry, every job recv begin/end (including disconnect), `compute` begin/end with index, result send begin/end, exit. Compute includes first scratch init, `decode_owned`, reconstruction, checksum verification and ownership transfer. |

`next` includes header parsing, Vec resize and buffered body read; [commit](../../crates/codec/src/frame.rs) includes cumulative limit, digest accounting and buffered write. These spans measure elapsed service, not syscall time. Frame prologue/footer, pool setup/join and CLI flush/launch lie outside block service. Record them as separate scopes or residuals.

## Reduce the trace by interval intersection

* **Admission and queues.** One credit is reserved *before* `next`; it becomes an admitted job on successful send and is recycled only after ordered commit. Track the coordinator's `admitted − committed` (0…8), plus any reservation in `next`/send. Account separately for the popped EOF buffer; it admits no job. Measure time at each occupancy and intervals from a failed free-pop to the first credit return. For actual admission pressure require a further **data** block, known post hoc from the complete frame, not merely an unread EOF. Split full-window time into head-wait, commit and other; it overlaps worker work.
* **Dispatch and collection.** Job send-begin → worker recv-return measures handoff plus queue residence and wakeup, distinct from worker recv-call elapsed or the coordinator's send duration. For the post-send portion use `max(0, recv_return − send_return)` as a lower bound on handoff latency; receipt may precede sender return. Use the same brackets on the result path. Compute-end → coordinator receipt includes send/scheduling/collection delay; receipt → commit-start measures pending residence. Report per-block distributions and pending peak/depth-time. Do not treat cumulative block-ms as elapsed stall.
* **Head wait.** Within the coordinator's missing-head receive loop, partition receive intervals by head state: (a) head still unfinished, no known later result; (b) head still unfinished, at least one later result complete; (c) head already complete, yet being delivered/collected. Split intervals *at* compute-end events. For (b) report both worker-complete evidence and its conservative subset with a later result already in `pending`. A slow `recv_result` can occur in (a); a late pending block can spend time behind earlier **writes** with zero (b). Sum (union) coordinator intervals once, regardless of how many later blocks finished. Keep loop bookkeeping separate. Describe (c) as collection/dispatch until a finer trace establishes cause.
* **Exposed ordering pressure.** Intersect (b) with no free credit, unread data, and workers in job-recv with no queued jobs. Report both wall-ms of the union and idle worker-ms in this intersection; identify blocking head indices, service times and count of stalled workers. Reconstruct queue nonemptiness from send/recv brackets; leave racing handoffs ambiguous (e.g., only call it empty when every send already begun has a matched recv-return). This is observed lost supply while order binds, **not** a predicted speedup: a slow head must still finish. Report drain-after-last-admission separately, even if EOF is not yet read.
* **Idle and concurrency.** Worker idle proxy = elapsed inside `recv_job`, including fast receives, wakeup and descheduling; split startup, refill gaps and terminal wait, and distinguish queue empty from work already offered. Result-send and other worker overhead are separate. For each frame take `T` = pool entry through completed join; show startup and drain separately and repeat intersections on the interior (first compute-start to last data admission, if nonempty). Integrate `n(t)` = number in `compute`: ms at 0…4, mean `∫n/T` workers, utilization `∫n/(4T)`. Reconcile `4T` with compute + recv + send + other live + not-yet-started/already-exited worker-ms. This measures elapsed overlap, not on-core occupancy. Coordinator fractions use `T`; coordinator time and worker time cannot be added as serial costs. Process CPU/wall gives mean occupied CPU cores across **all** threads; report clean CPU per plaintext GB and end-to-end wall too.

## Comparisons and gates for implementation

1. **Fix identity first.** Verify both archive and plaintext against the [SHA manifest](../../doc/profiling/pivco-worker-data/sha256.txt); cached inputs live under `.test-agent/pivco-worker/fixtures/`. Hash through EOF, require successful exit and quiet stderr per configuration. Use warm regular-file input and stdout redirected to `/dev/null` for timing. Record compiler, flags, binary hashes and CPU topology/affinity. Reproduce the four physical core mask (previously 2–5): four workers **plus** the coordinator share it. Keep SMT, other load, cache state and frequency policy comparable. `sao` is only ~14 blocks at 512 KiB versus ~98 on `mozilla`; do not pool blocks as independent runs or generalize fill/drain to steady state.
2. **Establish clean signal and probe cost independently.** Clean reference = retained-buffer source `530db0a0` or a recorded source-identical decoder, locked release CLI defaults, no ledger/Tracy/probes. Run clean serial and clean four-worker baselines on all four fixed cases; if explaining *the change due to reuse*, pair against clean `50cb0699` on the same inputs (tracing only the retained build diagnoses its current bottleneck). For probe cost, pair clean retained vs its traced twin, all four four-worker cases, identical flags/inputs/affinity. Include A/A clean pairs for noise; use discarded warmups and two independently balanced shuffled series of ≥12 adjacent pairs like the prior study. Save raw order, wall and `wait4` user+sys, median within-pair changes and uncertainty by series. Include sidecar cost in end-to-end overhead; if large, pair an otherwise identical **capture without dump** against clean to isolate in-pipeline perturbation. Compare traces from independent series. Predeclare a tolerance (suggest ±3% wall and CPU for capture, both series, with uncertainty narrower than the proposed effect); if cost, noise or trace instability masks the diagnosis, reduce probes/repeat or say inconclusive. Never subtract an average probe tax or use traced times for a speedup claim.
3. **Check the accounting.** Replay the existing two-slot blocked-head test with held job 0, then later job 1 pending, a free worker and job 2 still unread: positive conservative exposed wait, no premature admission. Contrast in-order slow head (generic wait only), later result retained while coordinator writes (residence only), last partial window (drain only). Use controlled edges or synthetic reducer records, not sleep thresholds. Require contiguous ordered commits, matching job/result counts and bytes, `0 ≤ admitted − committed ≤ K`, complete worker and coordinator budgets within clock resolution. Preserve timeout/error shutdown, ordered failures and limits in [scheduler tests](../../crates/parallel/src/scheduler_tests.rs).
4. **Choose one follow-up per supported mechanism.** If both series show interior idle with unread work during `next` or commit, measure that coordinator stage; isolate source/sink in a separate sensitivity run. If exposed order/credit idle dominates those gaps, use an existing clean slots sensitivity (e.g. 4/8/16, same four cores/workers) and check whether the gaps shrink *and clean wall improves* at acceptable CPU and buffer cost. If all workers usually compute and wait is mainly (a) or drain, inspect service skew by block/kind/bytes and independently profile the slow blocks' codec stages; elapsed compute can include preemption or memory contention. Split `sao` and long single-frame RAW are available no-Huffman controls. If signals differ by file, fall below the A/A noise, or fail to reproduce, report mixed/inconclusive and name the missing discriminator. Deliver per-case clean timings, trace budgets, exposure intersections, and representative head timelines. No scheduling or codec change is justified by residence, a wait count, or summed worker time alone.
